RECOVERED ANALYTICAL STATEMENTS

These proofs can be checked directly. They do not recreate lost experiment
outputs or establish why a learned representation changed.

1. COMMON ROW GRADIENT
Let P be a nonempty positive set, s_j finite logits, and w_j >= 0 fixed for
differentiation, with w_j = 1 on P. Define
  D = sum_j w_j exp(s_j), q_j = w_j exp(s_j)/D,
  y_j = 1[j in P]/|P|,
  ell = log D - sum_j y_j s_j.
Since P is nonempty, D > 0. Direct differentiation gives
  d ell/d s_j = q_j - y_j.
An individual positive gradient need not be negative when |P| > 1.
For q=(0.8,0.1,0.1) with the first two entries positive, gradients are
(0.3,-0.4,0.1). The total positive gradient remains nonpositive.

2. POSITIVE EXPANSION REDISTRIBUTES TARGET MASS
Assume all candidate weights are one. Fix scores and the candidate set.
Let K >= 1 original candidates be positive. Relabel M >= 0 already-present,
disjoint candidates as additional positives. The denominator and q stay
fixed. For an original positive p,
  g_expanded,p - g_original,p
    = (q_p - 1/(K+M)) - (q_p - 1/K)
    = M/[K(K+M)].
Each newly positive candidate changes by -1/(K+M); all others are unchanged.
This is a direct logit-gradient identity. It does not imply the same change
in actual scores after an optimizer step through shared model parameters.
In symmetric training the expanded set also changes eligible text anchors.

3. A CONSTANT CAN MATCH THE WEIGHTED NORMALIZER
For the same fixed positive set, let N be a nonempty set of nonpositive
candidates. Define
  c* = [sum_{j in N} w_j exp(s_j)]/[sum_{j in N} exp(s_j)].
The denominator defining c* is strictly positive. Replace every nonpositive
weight by c*, and hold c* fixed during differentiation. The total negative
exponential mass, D, row loss, every positive probability and positive
gradient, and sum of negative gradients are identical. Individual negative
gradients generally change because their allocation changes.
The executed empirical constant control used 0.25; it did not calculate c*.

4. TOTAL NEGATIVE MASS DOES NOT BOUND EVERY GROUP
For one positive and detached 0 <= w_j <= 1 on negatives, write unweighted
normalizer S and weighted normalizer D. Then D <= S and q_+ >= p_+.
Thus total negative gradient is 1-q_+ <= 1-p_+. This says nothing comparable
about each negative group. Let exponential masses be 1 for the positive,
1 for a chosen negative group, and 100 for other negatives. Give the chosen
group weight 0.5 and others 0.001. Its probability increases from
1/102 = 0.0098039 to 0.5/(1+0.5+0.1) = 0.3125.
Weights 2 used for contradiction hardening violate the <=1 premise.

5. SUMMED-POSITIVE PROBABILITY IS A DIFFERENT LOSS
For A = sum_{p in P} exp(s_p), define ell_set = log D - log A.
Its positive derivative is q_p - exp(s_p)/A <= 0 because D >= A.
This objective allocates positive attraction differently from uniform-target
cross-entropy. It was not trained in the completed study.

6. WEIGHTS THAT DEPEND ON PARAMETERS
When positive weights remain one and nonzero weights depend on theta,
  d ell/d theta = sum_j(q_j-y_j) d s_j/d theta
                 + sum_{j:w_j>0} q_j d log(w_j)/d theta.
The second term disappears for detached weights. Fixed-weight identities do
not establish bounds on this extra derivative or on the full encoder update.

7. ORIGINAL SINGLE-POSITIVE IDENTITIES AND CORRECTIONS
Original Lemma 2.1: ordinary softmax gradient p_j - 1[j=+]. Valid.
Original Theorem 2.2: a group of m negatives at common logit a and weight w
has gradient mass m*w*exp(a)/D. Valid identity; denominator also changes.
Original Theorem 2.3: let R=sum_negative(1-w_j)exp(s_j), 0<=w_j<=1.
Then D=S-R>0, and
  log q_+ - log p_+ = -log(1-R/S) >= R/S >= 0.
This changes a normalizer at fixed logits, not a raw retrieval score margin.
Original Corollary 2.4: a suppressed equal-weight group gives
R >= m(1-w)exp(a). A bound with denominator A+m exp(a) saturates as m grows;
it is not an unbounded linear scaling law.
Original Theorem 2.5: total negative mass and the magnitude of the positive
gradient do not increase under detached suppression. No training-stability
or parameter-gradient-norm guarantee follows.
Original Theorem 2.6: at equal logits, a smaller weight gives smaller q_j.
It does not establish semantic correctness of the weighting.
Original Proposition 2.7: weights approaching one recover ordinary loss by
continuity. Uniform negative weights below one do not recover it if the
positive weight remains one.
Shifted-logit form: weighted normalization equals ordinary normalization of
s_j+log(w_j), with zero weights represented by minus infinity. This is not
label smoothing, which changes targets.

The original 'safe by default' or 'cannot harm' interpretation was retired.
These identities leave scores fixed and cannot prove better rankings,
generalization, optimization stability, or a superior representation.
